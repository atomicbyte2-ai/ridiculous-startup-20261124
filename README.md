A one-sentence startup pitch for the make.fun bounty
[Ridiculous at First Glance, Real by the Second](https://make.fun/bounty/u-po-pu-ridiculous-at-first-glance-real-by-the-second-9a97d12a):
one strange idea, one payer, one reason the money works.

## The entry

> We rent out retired sniffer dogs as Airbnb security greeters that sniff guests' luggage for bedbugs, and hosts pay per stay to protect their 5-star rating.

**155 characters** — a single line, under the 200-character limit.

Why it works: bedbug-sniffing dogs are a real, trained profession, retired
police and customs dogs need second careers, and a single bedbug review can
destroy an Airbnb host's rating and bookings — so a small per-stay fee that
catches the problem at the door is cheap insurance, not a joke.

## How it runs

```sh
node main.ts
```

`main.ts` validates the sentence (single line, at most 200 characters) and
writes it to `entry.txt` inside `MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR` (default `/output`).
No network, no dependencies.

## Candidates considered

1. "We rent soundproof scream booths by the minute to open-plan offices, so stressed workers pay to yell, cry, and take calls without HR hearing a word." (148 chars) — vivid and plausible, but office pods already exist; the scream twist is the only strange part.
2. "We lease trained magpies to retrieve lost wedding rings from drains and gutters, and the panicked couples who lost them pay a finder's fee per rescue." (150 chars) — very bizarre, but training magpies to fetch specific rings on demand is the weakest second-look logic.
3. "We sell bottled fog to wedding photographers: dry-ice mist in a can that makes any backyard look like a misty Scottish moor for $19 a shot." (139 chars) — charming and specific, but a one-off prop, not a business with repeat payers.
4. "We run a dating service that pairs insomniacs for 3 a.m. phone calls, and lonely night-shift workers pay $9 a month for someone awake to talk to." (145 chars) — real loneliness, real night-shift market, but reads more sad than ridiculous.
5. **Winner:** "We rent out retired sniffer dogs as Airbnb security greeters that sniff guests' luggage for bedbugs, and hosts pay per stay to protect their 5-star rating." (155 chars) — the strangest image (a dog greeting you at check-in and sniffing your suitcase) with the sturdiest logic: the dogs, the skill, and the payer's fear of a ruined rating are all real, and the per-stay fee is a clear, repeatable revenue model.
6. "We run a drive-through confessional for atheists: licensed therapists in a booth hear your guilt in 10 minutes flat, and busy professionals pay $25 a session." (158 chars) — funny premise, but the drive-through framing fights the therapy logic instead of serving it.
7. "We breed glow-in-the-dark mushrooms as living night-lights for children's bedrooms, and parents pay $29 a pot for a night-light that never needs charging." (154 chars) — delightful image, but glow mushrooms are dim, slow-growing, and a weak substitute for a $5 plug-in light.
8. "We hire out professional line-standers to queue overnight for sneaker drops, and resellers pay $15 an hour because one pair flips for $400." (139 chars) — real arbitrage, but line-standing services already exist; not strange enough at first glance.

The winner won because it is the only candidate where every element of the
joke is also a real thing: retired sniffer dogs exist, bedbug dogs exist,
Airbnb hosts really do live or die by their rating — and the sentence makes
the money obvious in one breath.
